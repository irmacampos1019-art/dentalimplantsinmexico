All-on-4 vs 3-ON-8 Dental Implants: Which Full Mouth Restoration Is Best in 2026?

By Dr. José Moguel • Updated August 2026 • 12 min read

If you're missing most or all of your teeth, you've probably come across two major full mouth restoration options: All-on-4 and 3-ON-8. Both can give you a permanent, fixed set of teeth — but they're very different in design, cost, durability, and long-term results.

This guide breaks down everything you need to know to make an informed decision, including a head-to-head comparison of cost, procedure, recovery, and which option is best for your specific situation.

What Is All-on-4?

All-on-4 is a full arch restoration technique that uses four dental implants to support a complete set of fixed teeth. The two posterior implants are placed at an angle to maximize contact with available bone, often eliminating the need for bone grafting even in patients with significant bone loss.

Developed by Nobel Biocare, All-on-4 has become one of the most popular full mouth restoration options worldwide because it's relatively affordable, requires fewer implants, and can often be completed in a single day ("teeth in a day").

The prosthesis is typically made of acrylic teeth attached to a titanium or chrome framework. While functional, the acrylic can wear over time and may need replacement every 5-7 years.

What Is 3-ON-8?

3-ON-8 is a patented full mouth restoration protocol developed by Dr. José Moguel that uses eight dental implants per arch to support three individual screw-retained zirconia bridges. Instead of one large denture-style prosthesis, 3-ON-8 creates three separate bridge sections — two posterior sections and one anterior section.

This three-section design is the key innovation. It distributes bite force across 8 implants rather than 4, dramatically reducing stress on each implant. The zirconia material is also significantly stronger and more natural-looking than acrylic, and it doesn't absorb stains or odors.

Dr. Moguel has placed over 17,000 implants using this protocol over his 38+ year career, making him one of the most experienced full arch implant specialists in Mexico.

All-on-4 vs 3-ON-8: Detailed Comparison

1. Number of Implants and Stability

The most obvious difference is the number of implants: 4 vs 8 per arch. More implants means better force distribution. Think of it like a table — a 4-legged table is stable, but an 8-legged table is much harder to tip over.

With All-on-4, each implant bears roughly 25% of the bite load. With 3-ON-8, each implant bears only 12.5%. This reduced per-implant stress is why 3-ON-8 implants tend to last longer and have lower failure rates over time.

2. Prosthesis Material: Acrylic vs Zirconia

All-on-4 typically uses an acrylic prosthesis on a metal frame. Acrylic is lighter and cheaper but has drawbacks:

  • Wears down over 5-7 years and needs replacement
  • Can absorb stains and bacteria
  • May develop micro-fractures
  • Can look less natural close-up

3-ON-8 uses zirconia bridges, which are:

  • Nearly indestructible — zirconia is used in aerospace applications
  • Stain-resistant and hypoallergenic
  • Custom-shaded to match natural tooth color
  • Translucent, mimicking natural enamel
  • Rarely need replacement

4. Bone Requirements

All-on-4 is often the go-to choice for patients with significant bone loss. The angled posterior implants can be placed in available bone without grafting, making it suitable for patients who've been missing teeth for years.

3-ON-8 requires adequate bone density to place 8 implants. If you have severe bone loss, you may need a bone graft or sinus lift first. However, if you have good bone density, 3-ON-8 provides far superior long-term stability.

5. Recovery Time

Both procedures follow a similar recovery timeline:

  • Days 1-3: Swelling, minor discomfort, soft food diet
  • Week 1: Stitches removed, most swelling subsides
  • Weeks 2-4: Return to normal activities
  • 3-6 months: Implants fully fuse with bone (osseointegration)
  • 6 months: Final permanent prosthesis placed

With 3-ON-8, the initial healing may take slightly longer due to more implant sites, but the long-term stability is greater. See our after-care guide for detailed recovery instructions.

6. Long-Term Durability

This is where 3-ON-8 really shines. The combination of 8 implants and zirconia bridges creates a restoration that can last decades:

  • All-on-4: 15-20 years (acrylic prosthesis needs replacement every 5-7 years)
  • 3-ON-8: 25+ years (zirconia bridges rarely need replacement)

Over 20 years, an All-on-4 patient might spend $8,000 + 3 prosthesis replacements ($9,000-$15,000) = $17,000-$23,000 total. A 3-ON-8 patient spends $14,000-$18,000 with no replacements needed.

Which One Is Right for You?

Choose All-on-4 if you:

  • Have significant bone loss and want to avoid bone grafting
  • Have a tighter budget and need the lowest upfront cost
  • Want a well-established, widely-available procedure
  • Are okay with replacing the prosthesis every 5-7 years

Choose 3-ON-8 if you:

  • Have adequate bone density (or are willing to get a bone graft)
  • Want the most natural-looking, durable result possible
  • Prefer zirconia over acrylic
  • Want a long-term solution that won't need replacement
  • Are willing to invest more upfront for lower lifetime cost
